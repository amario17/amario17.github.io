About Me

Ph.D. candidate in Genetics and Plant Breeding at the Federal University of Viçosa (UFV), advised by Prof. Kaio Olimpio das Graças Dias, focusing on genomic prediction and the integration of genomic and environmental information to model genotypes × environments interaction and predict genotype performance in untested environments. I am currently a visiting scholar (sandwich Ph.D.) at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, advised by Prof. Alexander Lipka, developing statistical and computational models applied to plant breeding. My work spans quantitative genetics, mixed models, environmental covariates, multi-environment trial analysis, spatial modeling and the development of analytical tools in R, aiming to deliver robust solutions that breeding programs can put into practice.

Projects

Beyond Prediction: Quantifying Certainty in Genotype Performance Across Untested Environments
Ongoing

We are developing an approach to quantify the uncertainty of genotype performance predictions in untested environments, going beyond point estimates to support recommendation decisions with greater confidence.

From Multi-Environment Trials to Data-Driven Genotype Selection in Soybean
Ongoing

In partnership with GDM Seeds, we investigate what models that integrate environmental information actually capture of genotypes × environments interaction. Leveraging continental-scale data (5 breeding programs, 155 locations and 1,232 genotypes, from 2021 to 2023) and the broad representativeness of the company's germplasm, present in about 45% of global soybean production and in over 80% of the soybean area in Brazil, we seek to understand how these models represent genotype adaptation across space and what this means for selection in untested environments.

Tropical wheat expansion into the Brazilian Cerrado
Completed

How can we know where tropical wheat will perform well before it is even planted? We developed a pipeline that combines GIS, factor analytic models and 83 environmental covariates to predict genotype performance in untested environments, supporting the UFV wheat breeding program in expanding the crop across the Brazilian Cerrado. Pixel-by-pixel recommendations outyielded the best genotype indicated by conventional selection tools by 9.8% (rainfed) and 13.4% (irrigated). Published in Crop Science (2026).

Early selection of tropical wheat populations
Completed

Can we tell early which populations will give rise to the best lines? We evaluated 25 segregating tropical wheat populations in the F2, F3 and F4 generations across six environments, using models that treat generation as a nested effect and combine different genetic and residual (co)variance structures. The best model pinpointed the populations with the greatest potential to produce superior lines, making selection possible from the earliest generations. Published in Euphytica (2025).

qgtools: R functions for quantitative genetics
On GitHub

An open-source collection of functions I use daily in quantitative genetics analyses: two-stage multi-environment analysis (BLUEs, BLUPs, weights and Cullis heritability), climate and soil covariate assembly, and spatial interpolation via Random Forest.
